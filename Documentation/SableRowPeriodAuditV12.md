# Sable Row V12 — early-1950s American port-city audit

Audited 7 September 2026 against the user's confirmed setting. Scope is the current staged district, including the expanded buildings and the later freight/door revisions. Inspected the live visible-object inventory (7,339 objects grouped into 467 name families), material assignments, all 24 visible text objects, the full district renders and detail views. Repeated instances were assessed by construction family; this is not 7,339 independently authenticated historical objects.

## Result and correction

No clearly post-early-1950s technology or branding was identified in the current scene. One definite construction/material mismatch was corrected: **19 chimney pots were solid cylinders assigned the aged-oak material**, including the older objects named “Terracotta chimney pot.” Names did not match their actual materials. Rebuilt these as hollow clay flues, with matte fired-clay surfaces, visible wall thickness and dark sooted throats. Preserved their placement, outer dimensions, chimneys and roofs. This corrects an implausible construction detail, rather than removing a later invention.

The rest is plausible period scenery, with varying evidence strength. Exact makers, model years, street-light catalog numbers and municipal standards are not established by these stylized models. The fictional location also does not justify certifying regional details such as water-tank placement or a particular city's paving practice.

## Object-family decisions

| Family | Decision and evidence |
|---|---|
| Brick apartments, workshops and warehouses; masonry courses, parapets, recessed doors, stone thresholds, painted wood sash and transoms | Retain as older urban fabric. [NPS historic wood windows](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/wood-windows-history-s-eyewitness.htm) supports the window construction family. Door openings are now actual recesses; they are not contemporary frameless entrance systems. |
| Flat felt roofs, seams and patches, flashings, skylights and warehouse roof monitor | Retain. [NPS roofing history](https://www.nps.gov/crps/tps/roofingexhibit/roofing.pdf) documents composition/built-up roofing. These surfaces represent felt/tar roofing, not contemporary synthetic membranes. The glazed monitor is a generic older industrial construction, not a measured replica. |
| Chimney pots | Correct all 19 from sealed wood-shaded cylinders to hollow clay. The [1925 Atlantic Terra Cotta chimney-pot catalog record](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL24935739W/Chimney_pots_of_Atlantic_terra_cotta?edition=chimneypotsofatl00atla) supplies pre-period precedent. Our short plain flues are not copies of a specific catalog design. |
| Ornamental cast street lamps, opal globes and retaining collars | Retain. [LACMA](https://unframed.lacma.org/2018/02/06/story-urban-light) dates its recovered American street lamps to the 1920s–30s; [Los Angeles street-light history](https://lights.lacity.gov/about/history) documents their proliferation. Blender emission/point-light settings represent bulbs, not LED hardware. |
| Stoop fixtures, workshop gooseneck reflector, diner pendants and warm windows | Retain incandescent-family fixtures. Warm windows represent occupied rooms. [Smithsonian lighting history](https://americanhistory.si.edu/lighting/scripts/s20t.htm) also establishes commercial fluorescents by 1938, so fluorescent technology itself would not be anachronistic; no contemporary LED fixtures are modeled. |
| Two sedans, chrome trim, round lamps, whitewalls, divided glazing, vent panes, wipers and blank metal registration plates | Retain as generic postwar cars. [The Henry Ford's 1949 sedan](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/artifact/27335?AssetId=THF90472) and [Ford's 1949 history](https://corporate.ford.com/articles/history/the-1949-ford.html) support the broad styling. Object names “1950” and “1951” are not evidence of exact model-year accuracy. |
| Sectional workshop garage and industrial loading leaves | Retain. [Overhead Door history](https://www.overheaddoor.com/blogs/blog-detail/the-story-of-the-modern-garage-door) documents prewar upward-acting doors. The scene has no automatic sliding pedestrian-door controls or modern access panels. Loading leaves are generic framed industrial doors. |
| Wood-and-iron fire escapes, ladders, railings and stoop rails | Retain; [1936–37 NYC fire-escape poster](https://www.loc.gov/item/98516613/) establishes prewar precedent. No claim of modern code compliance or exact fire-escape engineering. |
| Wooden rooftop tanks, hoops, conical caps and steel supports | Retain as older infrastructure. [Rosenwach's history](https://www.rosenwach.com/company-rosenwach-tank.php) describes long-established wooden rooftop tanks with conical roofs. Their placement on these lower fictional buildings is a composition choice; hydraulic need is not validated. |
| Wood utility poles, crossarms, insulators and overhead wires | Retain traditional wood/porcelain/metal construction. No cellular antennas, satellite dishes, fiber cabinets or plastic conduit are visible. Generic prewar utility family; no exact utility-company specification claimed. |
| Diner enamel frontage, chrome edging, tables, booths, pedestal stools, crockery, menus, coffee urns and mechanical till | Retain. [1946 Library of Congress lunch-counter record](https://www.loc.gov/item/2002718649/) provides period precedent. [Formica's history](https://www.formica.com/en-gb/campaigns/history) documents decorative laminates before the 1950s, including restaurant tabletops by 1931. Plain laminate is appropriate; not all plastic-derived materials are modern. |
| Extract duct, metal vent caps and louvres | Retain simple fabricated metal equipment already corrected in V04/V05. No packaged modern rooftop HVAC plant is asserted. Exact ventilation engineering is not validated. |
| Ribbed galvanized refuse cans, rolled rims, lids and handles; steel yard drums | Retain ordinary metal containers. No wheelie bins, plastic recycling containers or contemporary logos. Earlier [V04 audit](SableRow1950sAuditV04.md) includes the contemporary federal metal-can reference. |
| Crates, braces, nails, consignment lettering and timber loading platforms | Retain conventional sawn-timber construction. [USF's cataloged 1950 loading-dock photograph](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/robertson_and_fresh/937/) supports the industrial setting; it is not an exact crate/dock blueprint. Lettering has no barcodes, QR codes or modern handling labels. |
| Asphalt, slab paving, stone kerbs and gutters, iron grates/manholes, iron bollards | Retain generic established materials. No tactile warning pads, modern road symbols or plastic street fittings. Individual joint/grate patterns are authored, not authenticated municipal designs. |
| Wood benches, trees, garden beds, planters, ivy, weeds, laundry and paper litter | Retain ordinary materials and street life. Seasonal planting and unreadable newspaper sheets do not establish an exact date. |
| All visible signs and numbers | Inspected actual FONT text: SABLE ROW, MOTOR SERVICE, DINER, COFFEE, OPEN, HARBOR PHARMACY, MERCER ROOMS, RADIO REPAIR, CORNER GROCER, BONDED STORAGE, MARINE ELECTRICAL, house number 24 and SR 210–221 consignments. No modern brands, URLs, phone-number formats, ZIP codes or QR/barcode markings. Plain lettering is plausible; font files are not claimed as historical type specimens. |

The blue mailbox visible in an earlier supplied concept is not an object in this Blender district. No mailbox recoloring was therefore necessary. For future additions, [USPS history](https://about.usps.com/who/profile/history/pdf/mail-collection-boxes.pdf) records olive-green collection boxes before the 1955 red/white/blue change.
